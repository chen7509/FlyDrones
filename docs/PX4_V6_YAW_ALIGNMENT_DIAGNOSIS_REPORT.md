# v6 PX4 yaw-health and source-mode diagnosis

The sealed v6 unarmed, supported-motion development capture's 1/251 healthy RGB-frame result has a concrete PX4 flag transition behind it. This read-only follow-up verified the original physical archive SHA-256 `30d968bbc295699f4a71f8b3b552e6eeaef829221f824a1795d666a22d2b7db6` and ULog SHA-256 `24a5f46b1bb8213b138bdd62b787e91579e8c48fb588d740c10dd716aaef4d05`. It did not rerun physics, OpenVINS, a controller, EGO or training.

All 51 logged `vehicle_status.arming_state` samples equal 1 (disarmed); all 34 `vehicle_land_detected.landed` samples equal 1. Across 34 sparse `estimator_status_flags` samples, `cs_mag_aligned_in_flight=0` and `cs_mag_3d=0` throughout; `cs_mag_hdg=1` in 25, `cs_yaw_align=1` in 27, and `cs_ev_pos=0` in all 34. The 3,059 local-position rows have only nine `heading_good_for_control=1` samples, from 3.172 to 3.236 seconds. The flag journal first shows `cs_yaw_align=1` at 3.172 seconds, GNSS position use at 3.180 seconds, and `cs_mag_hdg=1` at 3.244 seconds. There are no later heading-good rows. The prior [frame-level audit](PX4_V6_EKF2_CAPTURE_READBACK_REPORT.md) saw only one of these nine brief rows at its 10 Hz RGB sampling time, 3.2 seconds, sourced from GNSS.

The fixed PX4 commit `d6f12ad1c4f70ad3230afd7d86e971421e02fef4` sets `vehicle_local_position.heading_good_for_control` from `isYawFinalAlignComplete()`. Its `ekf.h` implementation requires `yaw_align` plus either no active magnetometer heading mode or completed in-flight magnetic alignment sustained over one second. In this ULog, magnetometer heading mode becomes active while `mag_aligned_in_flight` remains false, which is sufficient under the fixed-source condition to explain the observed loss of `heading_good_for_control` after the short startup window. The source's height-triggered yaw reset path additionally requires `in_air`; this run remained logged as disarmed and landed. This is a code/flag explanation of **the gate output**, not proof that a magnetometer fault, a specific height threshold or any single physical factor caused the lack of in-flight alignment. Sparse flags cannot rule out an unlogged short transition. The earlier [paired low/high-altitude probe](EKF2_HEADING_PROBE_REPORT.md) independently supports altitude-related in-flight alignment in different, armed development runs; it cannot be substituted for this v6 run.

The source files copied from the exact Git objects are `EKF/ekf.h` (`5c778ac613c42dfac76edc35b39e80128b068e8f`), `EKF2.cpp` (`f93f59085f9bd10aac312d6afee75d57927a72f7`) and `mag_control.cpp` (`d03229f7c2f9ff5e3a162d466843c9a931504780`). Each copied file's `git hash-object` matched the fixed object's ID. The v6 ULog and runtime identity remain as separately sealed in the original physical study; this report does not prove whole-binary/source equivalence or a calibrated magnetic model. PX4 source is BSD-3-Clause; no new dependency was installed.

This result clarifies two different next tests. An **unarmed receiver-only** VIO→EKF2 trial can inspect message reception, timing, innovations, rollback and `cs_ev_pos` while retaining its own unarmed safety gate; it must not claim an all-frame `heading_good_for_control` capture result. A later controlled **armed single-aircraft** trial, after sensor/clock/calibration and safety gates, must separately demonstrate sustained heading health and safe behavior before student training data or 5/20-aircraft expansion. The capture gate is not relaxed, and no v6 frame is promoted to training evidence. The 25-second OpenVINS offline accuracy screen and historical 0.873 RTF five-camera failure remain unchanged.
