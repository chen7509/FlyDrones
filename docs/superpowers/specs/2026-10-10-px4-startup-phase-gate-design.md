# PX4 startup phase gate before live TIMESYNC

## Intent and failure being addressed

The user needs an actual unarmed PX4/VIO/EKF2 evidence chain, not a timeout made easier to pass. The single frozen v10 run failed at 3 ms simulated time: an owned PX4 shell returned `never published` for `timesync_status`, then the 2 s cold-bootstrap progress guard expired before the PX4 log first showed Gazebo world readiness or the Onboard MAVLink endpoint. Preserve v10 as a failure. A new study may begin TIMESYNC only after proving that its own PX4 instance has completed the startup portion that creates the expected Onboard UDP link. The ordinary 2 s no-progress/no-source watchdog, 8 s TIMESYNC bootstrap, 25 s physical workload, safety gates, and 300 s capture supervision are not relaxed.

The current `CaptureWireOwner.bind()` constructs `ObservedWireSession` before `fixture.finalize()` and before the main stepping loop. Its cold 8 s and 2 s timers start at construction. The pinned PX4 `px4-rc.gzsim` waits for `/world/<world>/scene/info` before continuing; `rcS` sources `px4-rc.mavlink` later; that script creates the Onboard UDP link. This ordering explains why the UNIX command socket can answer while the MAVLink endpoint and `timesync_status` do not yet exist. It does not identify the PX4/Gazebo internal scheduling delay. The v10 observer reports first log visibility, not event occurrence. No data from v10 may be relabeled as a successful retry.

## Two phase contract

1. **Startup observation** begins at the actual PX4 spawn, with a fixed wall-clock cap no greater than the existing 60 s independent observer and 300 s capture supervisor. It may observe the child-owned fresh log and issue bounded, serial, read-only `mavlink status` queries through the same verified PX4 command socket. A nonzero/no-instance response remains an explicitly journaled pending startup observation, not a successful query or automatic retry of TIMESYNC. An unbounded command read, IO error, identity change, armed state, conflicting endpoint, source loss, log failure or deadline is terminal. Do not pause Gazebo lockstep or manipulate pose/sensors to wait. If existing 10 s source-start watchdog expires first, preserve that failure; do not extend it implicitly.
2. **Cold TIMESYNC bootstrap** begins only after a fresh startup log success marker and exact owned `mavlink status` endpoint proof. Record the `startup_ready_monotonic_ns`, process identity, source log offsets/hashes, raw status bytes/exit and separate transition journal **before** constructing `ObservedWireSession`. Its original 8 s absolute bootstrap window and 2 s progress/frame/request watchdogs start here and remain unchanged. The first `timesync_status` must still be exactly `never published`; if it is already published, refuse as a non-cold filter rather than resetting or stealing a warm session. The 500 modeled accepted sample requirement, original interval baseline/restore check, runtime/source/safety gates and no-network-ODOMETRY policy remain.

`fixture.finalize()` currently occurs after session construction and may be needed for PX4 to see the world. Integration must separate registration of the clock/PostUpdate callback from construction of the cold session, or prove a safe alternative ordering in a finite ordinary-process test. Any simulation steps before readiness remain counted in the same 25 s episode and logged as startup steps; no hidden warmup or discarded trajectory. Do not assert that finalization alone is enough to make the world available. No extra full physics run is authorized by this specification.

Use RED/GREEN, targeted and adjacent regression, changed Ruff, source/config hashes and independent review.

Only after the implementation and its declared runtime files are frozen may a distinct new study ID run **once** when resources are available.
